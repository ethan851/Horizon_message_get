## [First atmosphere detected on rocky exoplanet in habitable zone](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy4kdd1e0ejo) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Scientists using the James Webb Space Telescope have detected an atmosphere on the rocky exoplanet LHS 1140b, located in the habitable zone of its red dwarf star. This marks the first time an atmosphere has been confirmed on a rocky world in a habitable zone. This discovery is a major milestone in exoplanet science, as it opens the door to studying the composition and potential habitability of Earth-like worlds. It demonstrates that rocky planets around red dwarfs can retain atmospheres despite intense stellar radiation. The detection was made using transmission spectroscopy during the planet's transit, analyzing starlight filtered through its atmosphere. The JWST observations ruled out a mini-Neptune composition, confirming the planet is likely rocky with a volatile-rich atmosphere.

hackernews · neversaydie · Jul 17, 14:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48947560)

**Background**: Exoplanets are planets beyond our solar system. The habitable zone is the region around a star where temperatures could allow liquid water on a planet's surface. The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) can analyze exoplanet atmospheres by measuring the absorption of starlight as the planet passes in front of its star. This technique, called transmission spectroscopy, reveals the chemical makeup of the atmosphere.

## [Zilog Z80 Microprocessor Celebrates 50 Years](https://goliath32.com/blog/z80.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Z80 microprocessor, introduced in 1974, has reached its 50th anniversary, marking a milestone in computing history. The Z80 was a cornerstone of early personal computers and embedded systems, influencing generations of hardware and software; its longevity highlights its robust design and ongoing relevance in retrocomputing. The Z80 is binary compatible with the Intel 8080 but differs in flag register behavior and adds new opcodes; it powered systems like the TRS-80, ZX Spectrum, and Game Boy.

hackernews · st_goliath · Jul 17, 19:41 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48951461)

**Background**: The Z80 is an 8-bit microprocessor designed by Zilog, founded by Federico Faggin, who previously worked on the Intel 8080. It was widely adopted due to its low cost, single +5V supply, and enhanced instruction set. Its architecture inspired many later CPUs.

**Discussion**: Commenters shared nostalgic memories of learning assembly programming on Z80-based computers like the TRS-80 and ZX-81, praising the CPU's educational value. One user provided a technical correction regarding binary compatibility with the 8080, noting differences in the flag register.
