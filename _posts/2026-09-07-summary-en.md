## [Asahi Linux Now Officially Supports Apple M3 Chips](https://asahilinux.org/2026/09/m2-episode-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Asahi Linux announced official support for Apple's M3 chip, advancing its mission to run Linux on Apple Silicon. The milestone confirms M3 Macs can now use the project's kernel and drivers as a supported target. This move makes Linux a realistic choice on the latest Mac hardware, giving users more operating system options and strengthening the open-source ecosystem. It also demonstrates steady progress in reverse-engineering Apple's proprietary chips, which could eventually lead to broader Linux adoption on Macs. According to community feedback, early M3 support still lacks fully working sleep and HDMI features, which are seen as adoption blockers. As with other Apple Silicon support, GPU acceleration relies on the project's own OpenGL driver rather than Apple's Metal, affecting performance in some workloads.

hackernews · mdp2021 · Sep 6, 14:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49586698)

**Background**: Asahi Linux is an open-source project that ports Linux to Apple Silicon Macs by reverse-engineering their undocumented system-on-chips. It was founded by Hector Martin and targets a range of Apple chips, including the M1 through M4 families. Because Apple does not officially support other operating systems on its hardware, all hardware enablement in Asahi Linux must be independently developed and upstreamed to the Linux kernel.

This takes DLSS beyond simple upscaling and frame generation, making AI-based neural rendering a key part of in-game image creation. In addition to the hallucination-rate changes, a math score for Anthropic's Fable 5.1 was temporarily lowered by roughly 10 percentage points; OpenAI argued that the adjustments were meant to make the numbers represent the best estimate of model performance.
