## [Developer Apologizes for Apparent AI-Assisted Plagiarism of Open-Source Astronomy App](https://blog.terrygodier.com/2026/08/09/mea-culpa-dark-hours.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a blog post titled 'Mea Culpa – Dark Hours,' developer Terry Godier apologized after being accused of plagiarizing the open-source astronomy app Dark Hours. The apology follows his astrology app's rejection by Apple's App Store, after which he apparently used AI to recreate the project, even keeping the original name. This incident highlights growing concerns about AI coding tools enabling plagiarism at scale and raises questions about developer accountability and App Store policy enforcement. It also fuels broader community debate over whether AI-generated code should be subject to provenance review. The developer's cloned app replicated not only functionality but also the name and likely assets of the original Dark Hours project. Commenters noted that the apology omits any mention of or apology to John Gruber, who had written a retraction article on Daring Fireball after initially covering the situation.

**Background**: AI coding assistants like GitHub Copilot, announced by GitHub and OpenAI in 2021, can autocomplete code in development environments; OpenAI Codex, an AI coding agent released in April 2025, can write and fix code across CLI, web, and IDE integrations. These tools can generate entire projects from existing codebases, which, when misused, may lead to license violations and plagiarism. The open-source app Dark Hours is the original project, and its community is understandably concerned about the apparent unauthorized reproduction.

## [Everything You Do Is Being Recorded: AI Wearable Surveillance and Countermeasures](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/05/ai-wearable-surveillance-countermeasures/687203/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In May 2026, The Atlantic published an article examining the spread of AI-powered wearable devices that constantly record people's activities, and it surveys practical countermeasures such as adversarial patches, anti-facial-recognition makeup, and sousveillance tactics. This matters because ubiquitous AI wearables could normalize continuous surveillance, eroding personal privacy while empowering corporations and governments. The article's exploration of countermeasures signals an emerging societal arms race between surveillance and resistance, with implications for technology policy and civil liberties. The discussion references the University of Chicago Sand Lab's 'Jammer' project as early research underlying a company's product, and notes that modern facial recognition systems can adapt to some evasion methods. Practical defenses include CV dazzle makeup, IR LED glasses, and adversarial patches that fool computer vision models.

hackernews · ike_usawa · Aug 9, 11:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49230477)

**Background**: AI wearables are devices like smart glasses or body cameras that use artificial intelligence to record and analyze everything around the wearer. Surveillance is monitoring from above by authorities, while sousveillance - recording from below by individuals - is often proposed as a counterbalance. Adversarial patches are physical patterns designed to confuse computer vision systems, and anti-facial-recognition techniques such as CV dazzle use makeup or clothing patterns to disrupt face detection. These tools represent a technical pushback against increasingly pervasive and one-sided surveillance.

## [Moore Threads Plans Hong Kong IPO as H1 Revenue Jumps 147%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-ai-chip-designer-moore-threads-plans-hong-kong-listing) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Moore Threads, a Chinese AI chip company, announced plans to list in Hong Kong. The firm reported first-half revenue of 1.74 billion yuan, up 147% year-over-year, while its net loss narrowed sharply to 11.6 million yuan from 270.9 million yuan a year earlier. A Hong Kong listing would give Moore Threads access to international capital and global talent as it battles Cambricon and Huawei for market share in China's AI chip sector. It also highlights the revival of Hong Kong's IPO market, which has raised over $42 billion this year, the highest in six years. Moore Threads said the listing is aimed at deepening its internationalization strategy and attracting R&D and management talent. The company previously listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange at the end of last year, raising 8 billion yuan with a 425% first-day jump, and its shares have risen more than 420% since then.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 9, 11:05

**Background**: Moore Threads was founded in 2020 by former Nvidia executive Zhang Jianzhong, initially targeting the gaming and graphics rendering chip market before shifting to AI accelerators. AI accelerators are specialized processors designed to efficiently run machine-learning workloads, unlike general-purpose GPUs primarily used for graphics rendering. With U.S. export controls curbing Nvidia's sales in China, domestic players such as Moore Threads, Cambricon, and Huawei are vying to fill the gap.
