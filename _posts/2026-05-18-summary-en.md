## [GDS advises NHS to keep open source repositories open by default](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/17/gds-weighs-in/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The UK Government Digital Service (GDS) published guidance on May 14, 2026, recommending that public sector organizations keep open source code open by default, in response to the NHS's decision to close repositories after vulnerabilities were reported via Project Glasswing. This guidance reinforces the principle of open source in government, balancing security concerns with the benefits of openness and scrutiny. It could influence other public sector bodies facing similar security vs. openness dilemmas. GDS's guidance, titled 'AI, open code and vulnerability risk in the public sector', does not name the NHS but is widely interpreted as a rebuke. It states that making everything private adds delivery and policy costs, and reduces reuse and scrutiny.

rss · Simon Willison · May 17, 15:59

**Background**: The NHS had closed its open source repositories after Project Glasswing, an Anthropic cybersecurity initiative, reported vulnerabilities in NHS software. This sparked debate over security vs. openness. GDS, which sets digital standards for UK government, weighed in to reaffirm the open-by-default policy.
