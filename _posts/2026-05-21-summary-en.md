## [OpenAI Model Disproves 80-Year-Old Geometry Conjecture](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

An OpenAI model has disproved a central conjecture in discrete geometry, specifically the Erdős unit-distance problem, by constructing a counterexample using novel insights from algebraic number theory. This marks a milestone in AI-driven mathematics, demonstrating that large language models can not only assist but also produce groundbreaking mathematical discoveries, potentially accelerating research across many fields. The model used a formal proof assistant (Lean) to verify the counterexample, and the proof involved sophisticated algebraic number theory techniques applied to an elementary geometric problem. The result has been accepted by the community as valid.

hackernews · tedsanders · May 20, 19:05 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48212493)

**Background**: Discrete geometry studies combinatorial properties of geometric objects, and the Erdős unit-distance problem asks: what is the maximum number of unit distances among n points in the plane? The conjecture, formulated in 1946, stated an upper bound of n^(1+c/log log n), which the AI model disproved by finding a configuration exceeding that bound.

## [Google's AI Search Threatens the Open Web Ecosystem](https://tante.cc/2026/05/20/on-google-declaring-war-on-the-web/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google's AI Mode, also known as Search Generative Experience (SGE), now provides direct answers to queries using generative AI, often eliminating the need for users to click through to websites. This shift fundamentally changes the traditional search model where websites traded content for traffic. This change threatens the economic incentive for content creators to publish on the open web, as their work is repurposed without driving traffic. If websites block crawlers, the web could fragment into silos, reducing the diversity and availability of free information. Google's AI-generated summaries sometimes contain inaccuracies, as noted by website owners who see incorrect information from their own sites being displayed. The feature is part of a broader trend of zero-click searches, where users get answers directly on the search results page without visiting external sites.

hackernews · cdrnsf · May 20, 21:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48214449)

**Background**: Traditional search engines like Google index web pages and return a list of links ranked by relevance. Websites allow crawling in exchange for referral traffic. Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE) changes this by using large language models to compose a direct answer, often citing sources but not requiring clicks. This mimics other AI products like ChatGPT, posing an existential threat to the open web's traffic-driven model.
