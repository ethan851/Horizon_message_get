## [Digital game ownership is about control, not format](https://popcar.bearblog.dev/its-about-ownership/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article argues that the real issue in gaming is not physical versus digital media, but the loss of true ownership due to DRM and service models, where purchases are revocable licenses, not property. This challenges the gaming industry's shift to subscription and live-service models, as consumers increasingly demand the same ownership rights they have with physical goods. The article cites that Steam does not enforce hard DRM and allows offline play, but most platforms like consoles and subscription services impose continuous online checks and license revocations.

**Background**: Digital Rights Management (DRM) refers to technologies that control how digital content is used, often requiring online authentication. Games as a Service (GaaS) are games monetized over time through subscriptions or microtransactions, unlike traditional one-time purchases. This has led to debate over whether digital games are truly owned or merely licensed.

## [Hong Kong processes over half of China's chip imports, record high](https://thenextweb.com/news/hong-kong-china-ai-chip-trade-hub) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In the first five months of 2026, Hong Kong handled over half of China's chip imports, with re-exports valued at about $124 billion, accounting for 52% of China's total chip purchases—a record high. This solidifies Hong Kong's critical role as a key AI trade hub, impacting global semiconductor supply chains and highlighting geopolitical risks amid US-China tensions. AI-related electronics now constitute 57% to 70% of Hong Kong's exports, and the HKTDC has raised its 2026 export growth forecast to over 20%. However, Hong Kong's intermediary role also exposes it to significant geopolitical risks.

**Background**: Hong Kong is a free port with no tariffs, no capital controls, and an extensive air cargo network, making it ideal for high-value, lightweight, time-sensitive semiconductor trade. Its share of China's chip imports has grown from one-third a decade ago to over half today.

## [OpenAI Never Visited Stargate UK Site, Guardian Reports](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/04/openai-apparent-failure-visit-key-site-questions-stargate-uk-project) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Guardian investigation found that OpenAI never visited the core site of its Stargate UK project at Cobalt Park in North Tyneside, and local government officials never held meetings with OpenAI or partner Nscale. The project, announced with a $30 billion investment pledge, was suspended in April 2026 due to regulatory and energy cost concerns. This revelation casts doubt on the authenticity of high-profile AI investment announcements and may erode trust in OpenAI and UK government claims. It also raises questions about the viability of the UK's sovereign compute ambitions and the transparency of major AI infrastructure deals. The Stargate UK project was announced during a visit by former US President Donald Trump to the UK, touted as a flagship US-UK AI collaboration. Local Conservative MP stated that the project's realization now 'looks extremely unlikely'. The project was halted after citing the UK's regulatory environment and high energy costs.

**Background**: Stargate UK was a proposed multibillion-pound AI data center project announced by OpenAI, in partnership with NVIDIA and Nscale, aimed at strengthening the UK's sovereign compute capabilities. Sovereign compute refers to infrastructure that allows a country to run AI models on local data centers, ensuring data jurisdiction. The project was considered a major step in US-UK technology partnership.
