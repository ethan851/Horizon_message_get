## [Mojo🔥 Open Sourced Under Apache 2.0](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/18/mojo-is-now-open-source/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Modular has open-sourced the Mojo programming language, releasing the compiler and toolchain under the Apache 2.0 license. This follows the Mojo 1.0 release and the company's shift away from making Mojo a strict superset of Python. This milestone fulfills a long-standing promise made in 2023 and provides the AI/ML community with a high-performance, Python-inspired language that can target GPUs and other accelerators. By opening the source, Modular invites broader community contributions, potentially accelerating Mojo's adoption and impact. Mojo builds on the MLIR compiler framework rather than directly on LLVM, enabling it to target CPUs, GPUs, TPUs, ASICs, and other accelerators. It incorporates Rust-inspired features like static typing and a borrow checker, while maintaining Python-like syntax, and it is currently available for Linux and macOS.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 18, 21:39

**Background**: Mojo is a systems programming language created by Modular Inc., designed for high-performance AI infrastructure. It was originally intended to be a strict superset of Python, but that plan was revised around August 2025, with Modular noting that AI-assisted coding tools could help migrate Python code to Mojo. The language's use of MLIR allows it to leverage advanced compiler passes and produce highly optimized code for heterogeneous hardware.

## [China's Long March 10B achieves world-first net-catch rocket booster recovery](https://t.me/zaihuapd/43264) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On July 10, 2026, China's Long March 10B rocket launched from Hainan and its first stage was recovered by a net-based system on a floating platform at sea — the world's first net-catch recovery of an orbital-class booster. The recovery occurred about six minutes after stage separation, marking China's first controlled recovery of a rocket first stage. This milestone makes China only the second nation to recover an orbital-class rocket booster, after SpaceX, and introduces a novel net-catch method distinct from propulsive landing. It could accelerate China's reusable-launch efforts, lower launch costs, and reshape competition in commercial space. During descent, the booster deployed four hooks and was caught by a net stretched across the deck of a waiting recovery ship in the South China Sea. China plans to refly the very same recovered stage before the end of 2026.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 19, 00:16

**Background**: Reusable rockets lower launch costs by recovering and reflying the most expensive part — the first stage. SpaceX pioneered this by landing Falcon 9 boosters vertically on drone ships, while China's Long March 10B instead uses a ship-based net system to catch the booster after its re-entry and descent. The Long March 10 family is also intended to support China's future crewed lunar missions. This flight was the CZ-10B's maiden launch, and the recovery demonstrates an alternative to propulsive landing.

The essay, which sparked a 525-comment discussion, contends that paid placements increasingly crowd out organic product results. Because Amazon serves as a dominant product search engine for millions of shoppers, ad-driven distortions in search results can undermine consumer trust and sway purchasing decisions. The critique also feeds into wider debates about platform economics, advertising ethics, and potential regulation of marketplace search quality. Amazon's A9 algorithm ranks products based on factors such as relevance, sales performance, and customer behavior, but Sponsored Products ads—cost-per-click listings—can appear at the top of relevant search results. This means paid placements often outrank organic results, and Godin notes that even searches for a specific product can trigger ads from competitors.

## [Turbovec brings Google's TurboQuant vector quantization to Rust](https://github.com/RyanCodrai/turbovec) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Turbovec is a new open-source Rust implementation of Google's TurboQuant, an online vector quantization algorithm for compressing high-dimensional vectors. It aims to enable efficient vector search with low memory overhead, and community claims suggest 4GB is enough for 10 million documents. This matters because Rust lacks mature, memory-efficient vector search tooling, and quantizing embeddings can significantly reduce memory and cost for large-scale retrieval. It brings a recent Google research algorithm closer to practical production use and could influence the Rust vector database ecosystem. TurboQuant is based on the 2025 paper 'TurboQuant: Online Vector Quantization with Near-optimal Distortion Rate' by Google-affiliated researchers, and is scheduled to be presented at ICLR 2026. The implementation supports local vector search; community members note the README could be more human-friendly and look forward to SQLite bindings.

hackernews · fittingopposite · Aug 18, 18:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49349898)

**Background**: Vector quantization compresses high-dimensional vectors, such as embeddings, while preserving their geometric structure, which is essential for similarity search. TurboQuant is an online algorithm by Amir Zandieh, Majid Daliri, Majid Hadian, and Vahab Mirrokni that claims near-optimal distortion rate and works with related techniques like Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss and PolarQuant. In Rust, libraries like this can offer lightweight alternatives to heavier systems such as FAISS, which benchmarks show is no longer state-of-the-art.

## [Fixing a Bricked Framework Laptop with Low-Cost Tools](https://quantum5.ca/2026/08/16/fixing-bricked-amd-7040-series-framework-13-laptop-with-20-tools/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A detailed walkthrough shows how to revive a Framework 13 laptop with an AMD 7040-series CPU that was bricked by a faulty BIOS update, using only about $20 worth of tools. The author used an SPI flash programmer and pogo pins to rewrite the corrupted firmware chip. This matters because BIOS updates can brick perfectly working laptops, and many manufacturers offer no easy recovery path, pushing users toward specialized hardware skills. The story fuels ongoing debates about firmware update responsibility, warranty policies, and the right to repair. Framework chose not to populate a JSPI debugger header on the board for cost reasons, so the author had to use pogo pins to make contact with the SPI flash chip. The fix involved dumping the corrupted firmware and reflashing a valid image, a process that normally requires a dedicated SOIC clip and an SPI flash programmer.

hackernews · jp_sc · Aug 18, 13:18 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49345220)

**Background**: BIOS is the low-level firmware that initializes hardware before the operating system loads; if it is corrupted, the laptop may become unbootable, or 'bricked'. An SPI flash programmer can directly read and write the firmware chip, and a SOIC clip attaches to the chip's pins without soldering. Traditional BIOS recovery often uses a special USB drive, but when normal recovery fails, external hardware programming may be the only option. The right-to-repair movement argues that manufacturers should make such recovery procedures and parts more accessible to users.
