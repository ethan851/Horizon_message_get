## [Fight AI Bot Spam with Git's --author Flag](https://archestra.ai/blog/only-responsible-ai) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A blog post from Archestra describes how to use Git's --author flag to filter out spam commits and pull requests from AI bots in GitHub repositories. This technique allows maintainers to block contributions from known bot accounts by checking the commit author field. AI-generated spam is a growing problem for open-source projects, overwhelming maintainers with low-quality or malicious contributions. This simple, low-tech solution provides an immediate defense without relying on GitHub's policies or complex automation. The approach leverages Git's existing --author flag to filter commits by author name or email pattern, which can be used in CI/CD or pre-receive hooks to reject spam. However, it only works if bots use consistent and identifiable author information, and may accidentally block legitimate users with similar names.

hackernews · ildari · May 18, 15:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48181125)

**Background**: Git records a commit author and committer for every commit, visible via the --author flag. AI bots have been increasingly used to generate spam pull requests on GitHub, often with fake or generic author names. The --author flag allows maintainers to programmatically reject commits from suspicious authors before they are merged.

## [FBI Seeks Nationwide License Plate Data Access](https://www.404media.co/the-fbi-wants-to-buy-nationwide-access-to-license-plate-readers/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FBI is attempting to purchase nationwide access to automatic license plate reader (ALPR) data, which would allow them to track vehicle movements across the country. This raises significant privacy concerns, as it could enable mass surveillance of all drivers without judicial oversight, potentially violating civil liberties. The plan involves buying data from private companies that operate networks of ALPR cameras, such as Flock Safety, which collect and store vehicle location data in searchable cloud databases.

**Background**: ALPR systems use cameras and AI to capture license plate numbers and location data. They are widely deployed by law enforcement and private entities, creating vast databases of vehicle movements. Privacy advocates warn that such data can be used for tracking individuals without warrants, especially when aggregated across multiple jurisdictions.

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York reported that the unemployment rate for computer science graduates aged 22-27 reached 7.0% in the first quarter of 2026, significantly higher than nursing (2.1%) and special education (0.7%). Meanwhile, CS enrollment at Princeton and UC Berkeley has plummeted by 51% and 59% respectively. This data signals a potential cooling of the once-booming tech job market, which could reshape career decisions for students and influence university program investments. The decline in CS enrollment at elite institutions may also reflect broader concerns about job prospects in the field. The data comes from the New York Fed's quarterly labor market analysis. Additionally, traditional high-paying industries like finance and law are also cutting entry-level positions, with JPMorgan's internship acceptance rate dropping from 2.8% to 0.7% and PwC planning to reduce entry-level hiring by one-third.
